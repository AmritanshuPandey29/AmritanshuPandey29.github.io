Clinical Data Review Agent

Every week a clinical data manager opens five systems (patient records, visits, central lab, safety and data queries), compares them by hand and writes up what's wrong. I built an agent that does that legwork. It connects to all five systems, decides what to check next based on what it finds, and produces the review with draft site queries waiting for human approval.

# one run, simplified · synthetic trial: 12 sites · 300 subjects · 3 arms
edc.enrollment_by_site          → 12 sites, 300 subjects
lab.results_above_uln  ALT, 3×  → 7 subjects flagged
edc.get_subject       ×7         → 5 of 7 on the 20 mg arm
safety.subject_adverse_events ×7 → 0 matching AEs recorded
ctms.list_sites                 → 3 of 7 at a single site
queries.draft_data_query ×5     → held for human approval
# 32 tool calls · 5 MCP servers · 18 tools

What it caught

Seven patients with liver readings above three times the upper limit, none recorded as a safety event. That gap only shows up when lab and safety data are joined, which is exactly the check people skip under time pressure.

Guardrails I designed

  • Read-only everywhere except drafting a site query
  • Approval happens outside the agent, so it can't approve itself
  • Daily draft limit and duplicate checks
  • Lab connector shares patient IDs only, keeping the blind
  • Every number traceable to a tool call; every action logged

Why five connectors

Different teams and vendors own these systems, each with its own access rules. One server per system lets each have its own permissions and be swapped out without rebuilding the agent.

How I'd measure it

Review hours saved per week, real issues caught that people missed, and the draft approval rate as the quality signal before widening the agent's permissions.

The model was the easy part. The product decisions were what the agent may do, where a person must step in, and how to make every finding traceable.
